I’m making good use of my privileges here at the Air Force. They’re ample for those with the eyes to see them. Those who want to appreciate the world around them, that is. It’s a privilege to run when my sergeant wants me to run and ruck when my sergeant wants me to ruck and do push-ups my sergeant wants me to do push-ups. It’s a privilege to get as much sun as I do. It’s a privilege to get as much air as I do. It’s a privilege to be free from the distractions of the outside world. These will be privileges, it’s been made clear to me, I’ll always have. There’s another one I’ll have for only a short while. For one shift every day, while they’re stationed here with me, I’m privileged to stand at the end of a taxiway before a fence, which itself toes the gentle curve of a mountain pocked with growths between the gravel, three large bombers parked on the asphalt behind me. There seems not to be any clear rules around them yet, but they have weapons on them that I should not, even now, explicitly name. It’s a privilege that I can mention the beasts that hold them, at least. Someday, I won’t any longer be allowed to say that I know what these things are at all. I won’t even be allowed to reference them. For now, though, it seems to be alright. Some things are better to remind you how precious life is than others. A privilege it is indeed that I can love you more for the knowledge of how fleeting we make our time here to be, and how full of euphemisms and placeholders.

Be well.

Billy.
